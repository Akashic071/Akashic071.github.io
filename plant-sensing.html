M21 / PREPARATION FOR FINAL MASTER PROJECT

Plant Sensing

Work in Progress

What can sound and touch draw our attention to when we spend time with a plant? During M21, I explored this question with a Gloxinia, an ESP32, and handmade textile sensors as preparation for my Final Master Project.

Plant Radio

With Gloxinia as the plant I worked with, I used an ESP32 to explore how sensor inputs might be translated into sound. I wanted to see what a listening interaction might invite someone to notice around the plant.

The sounds in Plant Radio were design interpretations of prototype inputs. What those inputs can tell us about the plant’s physiology remains unverified.

Textile Sensors and Touch

Pompom Pressure Sensor

I used a textile pompom to explore how a soft, compressible form could serve as a pressure input.

Moisture-Sensitive Fabric

I explored how a fabric sample could respond to wetting as a possible input for an interaction.

Stretch Sensor

The stretch sensor prototype failed. It left me with a question for a future iteration about how to check whether repeated stretches produce comparable readings.

Touch Exploration

I explored touch as a way to begin an interaction around a plant and considered what kind of feedback could make that gesture feel meaningful.

Questions for the Final Master Project

Which inputs can I reproduce under the same conditions? How can the feedback make its connection to a gesture or a material change clear? These questions carry forward from the M21 prototypes into the next stage of the project.
