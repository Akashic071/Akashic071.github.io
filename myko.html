BIO-BASED TOY DESIGN / OPEN-ENDED PLAY

MyKo.

Designing Living Artifacts for Open Play with Mycelium

TU/e · M11 Project 2025 Group Project Children Aged 2–4

Overview.

What if children could grow their own toys instead of simply playing with finished products?

MyKo is a bio-based toy-making toolkit designed for children aged 2–4. Combining natural materials, mycelium growth, and open-ended play, the project explores how young children can engage with nature through hands-on making and sensory experiences.

Rather than providing a finished toy, MyKo invites children to participate in its creation, from collecting materials outdoors to shaping, observing, and eventually playing with their own mycelium-based objects.

Research & Exploration.

The design process combined benchmarking, semi-structured interviews with parents and relevant stakeholders, material experimentation, and iterative prototyping.

We explored how different natural substrates, including leaves, twigs, acorns, and chestnuts, affect mycelium growth, structural stability, and tactile qualities.

Through reflection and experimentation, our focus shifted from designing individual bio-based toys to developing a complete toy-making experience that incorporates material transformation and time.

The Making Journey.

MyKo encourages children to become active makers through a sequence of exploratory activities supported by adult supervision where needed.

01
Collect natural materials
02
Break and mix materials
03
Fill and shape the mold
04
Introduce mycelium
05
Observe growth and dry the toy
06
Play and return to nature

Heating, sterilization, and other potentially unsafe operations require adult supervision.

The Toolkit.

A set of tools designed to support the transformation from natural materials into self-made toys.

The final concept includes three key components: a multifunctional incubator, transparent toy molds, and a specially designed syringe for introducing liquid mycelium culture.

The incubator serves as a collection basket, growing chamber, and drying device at different stages. Transparent windows allow children to observe changes in the material over time.

DESIGN OUTCOME

Growing through Play.

MyKo proposes a process-oriented approach to early childhood play, where living materials, sensory exploration, and waiting become part of the making experience. The project explores how children might develop greater curiosity and awareness of natural transformation by participating in the creation of their own toys.
